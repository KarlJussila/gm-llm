# Session 1 — The Hook

## Narrative

1. **The Rusty Anchor, Greyhaven.** Dallid has been in town three days, asking about anomalies and Erasmus Venn. No one in Greyhaven has heard the name. He sits at a corner table with his spellbook and a half-eaten bowl of cold stew.

2. **Wren approaches.** A young human woman in a plain traveling coat with a Concordance badge at her collar crosses the room and sits at Dallid's table without invitation. She identifies him as "the Vedalken who's been asking about anomalies and missing researchers." She introduces herself as Wren, a Concordance worker, and offers a job.

3. **The job offer.** Wren describes the objective: investigate a magic-dead zone in the deeper tunnels of Hollowfell Mine, a mineral extraction site in the foothills east of Greyhaven. Spells fail in the deeper tunnels — even cantrips produce nothing. A worker vanished last week. The mining consortium is hemorrhaging money. The Concordance has no one available to send. Wren does, if Dallid is willing.

4. **Dallid probes.** Dallid asks about the Briarwood and Erasmus Venn specifically. Wren corrects him: "Wrong mine." The Briarwood is her assignment, not his. She says she doesn't know the name Erasmus Venn but can check with the Concordance archives.

5. **Wren's Briarwood assignment.** Wren's official scope is monitoring temporal distortions in the Briarwood — time moves differently inside the forest. A research team went to the Echo Glade three days ago and hasn't returned. She confirms the Briarwood has been "wrong for months" — people go in and come out different, or don't come out at all.

6. **Dallid reveals his connection.** Dallid tells Wren that Erasmus Venn was his mentor, that he disappeared a year ago, and that his last known trail leads to the Briarwood. He says the Concordance erased Erasmus's records after his disappearance. Wren doesn't recognize the name but is visibly recalculating.

7. **Dallid accepts the Hollowfell job.** Despite the Briarwood being his primary trail, Dallid accepts. He asks for any information Wren can provide about the Briarwood anomalies.

8. **The map.** Wren produces a hand-drawn map marking three Briarwood anomaly locations: the **Deep Paths** (ancient trails that lead places that shouldn't exist), the **Echo Glade** (a clearing where sounds from the past surface), and the **Thornwall** (a barrier at the forest's edge that grows denser when approached with hostile intent). She says the missing research team was heading for the Echo Glade.

9. **Wren's parting.** Wren tells Dallid to report to **Brennan Cole** at the mine. She adds, unprompted at the end: "One thing I didn't say earlier. Seraphine Duskward — she's the one who sent me to Greyhaven. She's the First Warden of the Concordance." She leaves.

10. **Maret's directions.** Dallid asks Maret (the tavern keeper) about travel to Hollowfell. She tells him: a few hours east, follow the old mining road past the bramble line into the foothills. Safe at night if you know the road — the bramble is worse during the day when the light shifts. You'd want a lantern and to move steady.

11. **Departure.** Dallid settles his tab, folds the map into his coat, tucks his spellbook into his satchel, and departs Greyhaven at night. His two-headed gecko companion sits on his shoulder.

12. **Travel.** Two hours east. The road steepens. Marsh gives way to rocky ground, then foothills crowned with scrub pine.

13. **Arrival at Hollowfell Mine.** The surface works are spartan: timber frames, ore carts, bunkhouses with smoke rising. Workers move with the slow gait of hard labor. They glance at Dallid but no one speaks. The entrance to the deep tunnels gapes in the hillside — timber supports framing darkness. A cold draft flows outward, carrying the smell of damp stone and "a wrongness, a vacancy, like the air itself has been hollowed out."

14. **The dead zone.** The dead zone begins at the tunnel mouth. Dallid feels it — a threshold where ambient magic simply stops. Cantrips would fail. Wards would unravel. The cold is not natural; it is "the cold of absence."

15. **The spellbook reacts.** Dallid's spellbook stirs — not pages in a draft, but a deeper vibration, a resonance, as if the book is trying to align with something it can sense but Dallid cannot. The reaction is involuntary and persistent. The book *sees* something in the dark.

16. **Session ends** at the tunnel entrance. The dead zone is palpable. The spellbook is stirring. The night is dark.

## Engagement

**What the player leaned into:**
- Wren's introduction — the professional directness, the slight crack when she mentioned the Concordance's resource limitations
- The layering of mysteries — Hollowfell as a separate job, the Briarwood as his own trail, the suggestion they might be connected
- The spellbook cliffhanger — sensory details (cold of absence, hollowed air) rather than mechanics
- Probing questions before committing — the player played Dallid as analytical and precise

**What the player flagged as slightly off:**
- The map Wren provided felt too convenient — she handed over detailed Briarwood anomaly information to a stranger she'd just met. The player suggested she could have been more guarded or offered it as a trade (Briarwood data for Hollowfell findings), reinforcing her professionalism and making the relationship more transactional.
