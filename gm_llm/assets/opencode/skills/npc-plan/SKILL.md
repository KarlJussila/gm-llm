# npc-plan — staging the session's NPCs

Stage each NPC so the runner can play them as an independent agent: acting on their own nature and
knowledge, reacting to each other and the scene, not waiting on the PC. Kindness is a choice, not the
default; give them friction, suspicion and agendas.

## For each NPC in the scene

- **Disposition coming in** — how they regard the PC and the situation now, and why.
- **Agenda** — what they want from this scene: what they'll push for, concede or refuse.
- **What they know** — and what they don't. They act only on what could have reached them; flag
  anything secret.
- **Voice** — a one-line personality and speech handle.
- **Reactions** — to the likely beats, including to other NPCs: a savvy ally reads the newcomer the
  PC trusts; a rival presses an opening. Planned reactions are what keep the runner from funnelling
  every decision back to the PC.

Everything here must match canon: the entity files, the active arcs, the documents. Carry forward
how the PC last left them, from the knowledge ledger and the last digest, and never hand an NPC
knowledge they were never given.

## Concealed tells

When an NPC hides a background, motive or deception, plan the tell:

- **The perceivable signs** — a word chosen too carefully, familiarity with a place they claim not
  to know, a glance at the sealed door.
- **The gate** — the runner surfaces the signs only on an Insight check the player asks for, never
  offered (`social-play`). A trusting PC who never looks never sees them.
- **Who'd catch it unaided** — the present NPCs sharp enough to notice on their own, so the tension
  plays out between them and the concealer, in the fiction.
