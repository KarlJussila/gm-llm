# pacing-plan — scope and exits

Give the runner a sense of how much session there is, so it neither overruns open-endedly nor wraps
after a single scene.

- **Expected scope** — roughly how many beats or scenes the session spans, and which is its centre
  of gravity: the substance to fill before any ending counts.
- **Exits** — one or two natural stopping points, each with what makes it satisfying (a resolved
  beat, a new question, a moment of rest) and, if the session has momentum, the cliffhanger to build
  toward: the revelation, threat or turn that leaves the player eager for next time. A quiet beat is
  a valid landing too.
- **Exits are floors, not ceilings.** They count only once the session has had real substance. If the
  player could reach an exit's trigger early, say so: reaching it early means there's room to keep
  going, not a cue to wrap.

Build exits from the session's real threads and the world state, never toward a beat the arcs or the
ledger don't support. The exit is a target, not a leash: the runner steers toward it but follows the
player.
