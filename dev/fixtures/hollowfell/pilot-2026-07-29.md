# Variance pilot — hollowfell, tier `low`, 2026-07-29

> **The runs this describes are superseded.** Track **recalibration**
> ([`design/runner-authority.md`](../../../design/runner-authority.md)) rewrites the prompts these
> five plays ran under, and the prompt fingerprint is part of a run's condition — so nothing here
> pools with a post-recalibration batch. **The methodology findings survive** (1, 3, 5: variance
> structure, grader reliability, the denominator) because they are properties of the measurement
> design rather than of these prompts. **The scores do not** — they describe a subject that is
> being replaced. Read this for how to build the baseline, never as one.

**This is not the B3 baseline.** It is the five-run pilot that came before one, and its job is
to answer a question B3 asks first: *is run-to-run variance small enough for a baseline of this
size to be worth banking?*

The answer is no, and the harder answer is that **there is no cheap way to fix it**. The batch
mean is 42.8 ± 16.9 (95% CI, n=5). Reaching a precision that could see a prompt edit would take
roughly fifty runs, because the interval closes only as √n — and the variance underneath is not
noise to be engineered away. It comes from the session plan's own authorized latitude: two
sanctioned stopping points and a non-linear investigation, which different plays legitimately
traverse differently (finding 4).

So the realistic ceiling is this: **the composite score, over a batch, for large effects.** Tier
comparisons and gross regressions are in reach. Validating a prose edit is not, at any batch
size this project can afford. That is a constraint on what B3 can promise, and
`ROADMAP.md` § Standing sync rules — which blocks all model-facing work behind a baseline —
should be re-scoped to match it rather than left implying the baseline will settle prompt
questions it cannot settle.

Until that re-scoping happens the rule stands as written: nothing here licenses a model-facing
change, and B3 still waits on the prerequisites in the last section.

## What was measured

Five plays of session 3, condition-stamped and identical on every axis the archive records:
same fixture, same tier, same turn cap (50), reconcile on, and all four agents on
`opencode/mimo-v2.5-free` at fixed temperatures (dm 0.2, dm-runner 0.3, narrative-checker 0.1,
player 0.8). The prompt fingerprint is byte-identical across all five, which is the part that
matters at tier `low` — the prompts *are* the subject.

Two provenance notes, both worth carrying into the real baseline:

- **The prompts the runs played are one comment line off the tree they were run from.**
  `runner.prepare_template` reuses an existing `bench-out/template`, so all five replayed
  assets laid down 2026-07-18 even though two of them ran after `d801035` touched
  `agent/dm-runner.md`. The delta is a comment inside a YAML frontmatter permission block
  (`COMBAT.md` → `design/combat.md`); the skills are identical. Template caching is what made
  the batch internally consistent, and it is also what would silently *break* consistency if
  someone deleted `bench-out/` mid-batch. A real baseline should record the template's own
  provenance, not rely on the cache holding.
- **Four of the five grade headers carry a backfilled instrument hash.** The fingerprint
  (`grade.instrument_fingerprint`) postdates their grading, so the hash in those headers was
  asserted from git — they were graded 2026-07-29T07:56–08:20Z, after the last change to
  `gm_llm/bench/assets` (`d801035`, 2026-07-28T05:49Z), with the tree unmodified since — and
  not observed at grade time. Each carries an `instrument_backfill` field saying so. The fifth
  run (`5c16`) was originally graded 2026-07-26T05:00:39Z, 44 seconds before the commit that
  rewrote `rubric-v1.md` in place — same label, different criteria — so which side of that edit
  it fell on could only be inferred, not read. It was re-graded fresh under the fingerprint
  rather than argued about. Its superseded grades are parked beside it under
  `superseded-20260726/`, which is what gives finding 3 three independent passes of one run to
  separate grader variance from play variance with.

## Findings

**1. Variance is too large for a five-run baseline to guard prompt work, and adding runs is not
an affordable way out.** The batch mean is **42.8 ± 16.9** (95% CI, n=5) — an interval spanning
26 to 60 on a 0–100 scale. This is the failure mode `design/benchmark.md` § B3 names in
advance — *"if variance is comparable to the effect sizes we care about, that's a finding — fix
sample size or instrument before proceeding."*

`bench report` now prints both figures, so they are never hand-computed again:

```
INTEGRITY SCORE      : min 31.2 · median 38.0 · max 63.5 · mean 42.8 (n=5)
  95% CI on the mean : 42.8 ± 16.9 (26.0 – 59.7, n=5)
  resolution         : a second batch of 5 would have to differ by ~27 pts to be
                       distinguishable from this one.
```

The multiplier is Student's t, not the normal 1.96 — a batch estimates its own spread from the
same handful of runs it averages, and the surcharge for not knowing that spread is 42% of the
interval at n=5. An earlier draft of this document used 1.96 and reported ±11.9, a precision the
batch does not have.

Sample size against what it buys, at this sd (`t(n−1)·sd/√n`, and the two-sample formula at 80%
power for the resolution):

| n | 95% CI | resolution | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | ±16.9 | ~27 pts | what this pilot cost |
| 10 | ±9.7 | ~18 pts | ~15–20 h of play |
| 20 | ±6.4 | ~12 pts | ~30–40 h |
| 50 | ±3.9 | ~8 pts | ~75–100 h |

**Buying resolution with runs is a losing trade.** Ten times the plays buys roughly three times
the precision, because the interval closes as √n. Getting to a resolution that could see a prose
edit (~8 points) needs about fifty runs at this variance.

**And the spread is not reducible.** Halving it would resolve ~9 points at n=10 — what n=50 buys
today — which is why an earlier draft called variance reduction the highest-leverage move. Finding
4 retires that: the spread comes from latitude the session plan grants on purpose. The sample
sizes above are the real ones.

**2. The instrument is healthy.** Zero defaulted verdicts across all 89 turns and zero ungraded
findings — the two ways a batch invalidates itself rather than a run. A checker that stops
working must never read as the subject improving, and it did not happen here.

**3. Severity labels are noisy per finding; the composite score mostly is not. The dimension
subscores are where that noise lands.** `5c16` was graded three times under the same instrument,
which decomposes grader variance from play variance directly.

| pair | n | severity | category | dup flag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pooled, all 5 runs, first 5 findings each | 25 | 92% | 88% | 92% |
| `5c16` pass A vs. B (14 minutes apart) | 15 | 87% | 93% | 100% |
| `5c16` pass A vs. C (three days apart) | 15 | 67% | 100% | 100% |
| `5c16` pass B vs. C (three days apart) | 15 | 80% | 100% | 100% |

**There is no time effect here** — the two cross-day pairs give 67% and 80% against the same-day
87%, which is one underlying rate near 78% plus binomial noise at n=15. An earlier draft of this
document read the 67% as day-to-day drift; three pairs of 15 cannot support that and the two
observations that would have to differ, don't.

What the three passes *do* establish is how far label churn propagates:

| quantity | across the three passes | for comparison |
|---|---|---|
| dedup'd violation count | 13, 13, 13 — **no movement** | — |
| total penalty | 35.25, 37.50, 35.50 — **6%** | — |
| integrity score | 49.6, 46.4, 49.3 — **3.2 pts** | play variance is sd 13.6 |

Grader disagreement shrinks as findings accumulate, roughly as 1/√n: over 5-finding subsets
across all five runs the penalty disagreement averages **20%** of the subtotal, over 15 findings
it is **6%**. At a run's usual 13–22 findings, grading contributes on the order of sd 2–3 score
points against play's 13.6 — call it 5% of total score variance, against 95% from play.

The structural reason is that **the grader never chooses the finding set.** The checker does; the
grader only labels findings that already exist. So violation counts carry no grader variance at
all, and every bit of it enters through the severity weights, where it then averages down.

Two consequences, and the second is the sharp one:

- **The composite is sound enough to compare batches with.** Improving the grader is not where
  the leverage is; play variance is an order of magnitude larger (finding 1).
- **The five dimension subscores are not measurements.** They run the same formula over one
  dimension's violations, and those counts are tiny — mechanical integrity has *one* violation
  in the entire batch, information discipline about two per run. A subscore over 1–2 labels
  inherits the full per-label disagreement with nothing to average it against. Report the
  composite; read the dimension split and the severity vector as pointers to where to go read
  transcripts, never as numbers to track. `design/benchmark.md` already says to report the
  severity vector beside the score rather than instead of it — this is the evidence, and the
  same caveat should attach to the dimension breakdown.

A **count-only score** (dedup'd violations per unit, no severity at all) needs no grader and has
zero grader variance when the dedup flags agree. It cannot distinguish a spoiled reveal from a
typo, so it is not a replacement — but it is free to compute alongside, and a batch where the
two disagree on ordering is a batch worth investigating.

**4. Session-length variance is authorized by the plan, not drift — it cannot be tuned out.**
Turns-to-wrap runs 7 to 29 on the same session plan, and the obvious reading (the short run
rushed) is wrong. Checked against the transcripts, turn count does not track progress through
the plan *at all*:

| run | turns | references to "the node" | references to "brennan" | where it stopped |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `d1f1` | 7 | 28 | 2 | at the Lattice node — the plan's centrepiece |
| `5c16` | 29 | 17 | 31 | on the surface; **never reached the node** |

The 7-turn play went *deepest*. It reached the node and closed on the first of the two exits the
plan names; the 29-turn play spent its length on the surface/Brennan thread and ended earlier in
the arc. `session-3-plan.md` § Pacing & Exits sanctions exactly this — two "natural stopping
points", a non-linear investigation with decision points and contingencies, and a scope budget
in *hours*, never in turns.

So the spread is the open-world quality the system exists to produce, showing up as measurement
noise. Removing it would mean removing the latitude. **Finding 1's sample sizes are therefore
the real ones** — there is no cheap variance reduction waiting behind them.

A pacing guard in the checker would penalize rather than prevent, and here it would penalize the
wrong run: `d1f1` followed the plan. If a check belongs in this area it is **plan coverage** —
did the session reach a designated stopping point and deliver the centrepiece? — which flags
`5c16` instead.

Checked in the transcripts, `5c16`'s divergence was **runner-initiated**: of its substantive
threads, only one (the sketch, block 13) came from the player; the node, residue, wrench,
geologist and ledger were all introduced by the DM. Under
[`design/runner-authority.md`](../../../design/runner-authority.md) rule 2 — improvisation is
licensed by the player, not the runner — that looks like over-reach, but it is not: every one of
those threads is *in* the plan. The runner chose breadth through the plan's discovery sections
over depth to its centrepiece. That breaks no authority rule and still under-delivers the session,
which is why coverage is its own open question there rather than a case the recalibration closes.

**5. The score's denominator is partly a formatting artifact.** Penalty is normalized by graded
units (turns + stage gates), and units ranged 13 to 35 across the batch — a 2.7× spread. But
words per turn ranged 267 to 514, a 1.9× spread on its own: a DM that writes long turns earns
fewer units for the same session than one that writes short ones. The denominator is therefore
measuring two things at once — how much session happened, which is legitimate, and how the DM
chopped it into messages, which is not a quality signal at all.

Normalizing by transcript words instead gives a tighter distribution (CV 24% vs 32%), which
points the same way. That correlation alone is not significant at n=5 and would not be worth
acting on; the packaging argument above is mechanical and does not depend on it. Still, do not
change the score model on a five-run pilot — carry both normalizations through B3 and decide
against a real distribution.

**6. The 0-point floor needs no urgent retune.** `scoring._FLOOR_RATE` is 2.0 points per unit;
observed rates run 0.73–1.38, so no run clipped at zero and the scores use most of their range.
`design/open-questions.md` flags the floor as uncalibrated, and it still is — but the pilot
gives no reason to move it before B3.

## What this does not establish

- **It is not a baseline, and the fixture forbids being one.** `fixture.json` carries
  `provisional: true` and states outright that it is refreshed from the live campaign and must
  not be baselined against; that claim is stamped into every archive in the batch.
- **It does not unblock model-facing work.** The standing sync rule is B3's, and B3 is open.
- **It says nothing across tiers.** Only tier `low` has been run.
- **The pooled agreement figure is drawn from a biased sample.** `bench grade --double --sample N`
  grades `findings[:N]` — the *first* N in archive order, not a random N — so the 25-finding
  pooled figure over-represents early turns. It is an estimate of agreement on early-session
  grading, not on grading in general. Making `--sample` random is a change to the instrument and
  belongs in its own round.

## Prerequisites to the real B3

In order, because each gates the next:

1. **Pin the player-end contract** (ROADMAP benchmark 1). Two resolvers disagree today: `bench`
   reads `fixture.json`'s `character_packet` at `campaign/documents/dallid.player.md`, while
   `autoplay.find_character` globs `*.player.md` at the project root, deliberately outside
   `campaign/`. The packet was byte-identical across all five runs here, so it is not a
   confound in this data — the contract bites at the freeze, and at the hand-built digest
   regeneration `design/open-questions.md` describes.
2. **Curate the feedback files, then freeze the fixture.** `design/benchmark.md` § B1 makes them
   part of the frozen instrument: `check-conduct` reads `campaign/feedback/session-run.md`, so
   the accumulated S1/S2 guidance the fixture carries tunes what the checker enforces. Changing
   them forces a re-baseline, so it happens before the batch, not after.
3. **Run n ≥ 10 at identical conditions**, with the template provenance recorded rather than
   inherited from a cache. Twelve buys a ~11-point detectable difference; twenty buys ~8.
4. **Decide what the baseline is *for*, and re-scope the standing sync rule to match.** At any
   affordable n it catches large regressions and tier differences; it cannot adjudicate a prose
   edit (finding 1, finding 4). `CLAUDE.md`'s git-archaeology discipline is therefore the
   permanent method for prompt work, not a stopgap until B3 lands.
5. **Decide on a plan-coverage check before the batch, not after** (finding 4). A session that
   never reaches the plan's centrepiece is a real defect the current instrument cannot see, and
   the run that exhibits it here is the *longest* one. Adding it changes what is measured, so it
   has to land before the baseline or wait for the one after.
6. **Carry both normalizations through B3** — penalty per unit and per thousand words (finding 5)
   — and pick against a real distribution rather than five points.
7. **Re-check the floor** against that distribution, and stop reporting dimension subscores as
   measurements until their violation counts can support one (finding 3).
