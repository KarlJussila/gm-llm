# Session 1 — transcript

## DM

The Rusty Anchor smells of peat smoke and river mud — the familiar scent of a frontier town that has learned to live with damp. Timber beams dark with age sag above the common room, and the fire in the hearth throws more smoke than warmth. The last of the autumn light bleeds through windows caked with months of grime.

You've been here three days now. Your spellbook sits open at the corner table, its pages shifting faintly in a draft you can't feel, alongside a half-eaten bowl of stew that has gone cold. You've been asking questions — about the anomalies, about Erasmus Venn — and getting the same answer from everyone: blank looks and shaken heads. No one in Greyhaven has heard the name.

The tavern keeper, a weathered woman named Maret, wipes the bar with a rag that has seen better decades. A few dockworkers nurse their drinks in silence. Near the door, a young human woman in a plain traveling coat has been sitting alone for an hour, nursing a single ale and watching the room with the kind of attention that doesn't belong to someone enjoying a drink. Concordance badge, barely visible at her collar. She's been glancing at you — not subtly, but not intrusively either. Watching.

Now she stands. She crosses the room with purpose, stops at your table, and looks down at you with professional directness.

"You're the Vedalken who's been asking about anomalies and missing researchers." Not a question. "My name is Wren. I work for the Concordance." She pulls out the chair across from you without waiting for an invitation and sits. "I have a job. Interested?"
## Player

I lean forward slightly, my pale eyes fixed on her Concordance badge as I note the specific phrasing she used. "Anomalies and missing researchers," I repeat, letting the words hang. "The anomalies in the Briarwood specifically, and the researcher Erasmus Venn."

My fingers tap once on the table. "I am interested. Describe the job. What is the objective, the location, and the expected dangers? And how does the Concordance define 'anomalies' in this context?"

## DM

Wren's eyes flick to the badge — a quick, involuntary touch — then back to you. She doesn't correct the assumption about the Briarwood.

"Wrong mine," she says. "The job is at Hollowfell — a mineral extraction site a few hours east of here, in the foothills. The Briarwood is my assignment, not yours." She pauses, and something shifts in her expression — a brief crack in the professional veneer. "Hollowfell has a magic-dead zone in the deeper tunnels. Spells fail. Wards fail. Even cantrips produce nothing. A worker vanished last week. The mining consortium is hemorrhaging money and the Concordance—" She stops. Recalibrates. "The Concordance doesn't have anyone available. I do, if you're willing."

Her hand drops from the badge. She leans back in the chair, but her posture stays alert — the kind of stillness that comes from habit, not relaxation.

"Objective: find out why magic fails in the deeper tunnels. Location: Hollowfell Mine, foothills east of Greyhaven. Expected dangers: unknown — that's the problem. The workers report strange sounds, a worker disappeared, and no one can get close enough to the dead zone to study it with magical tools." She meets your eyes. "That's what I have. I'm not going to pretend I know more than I do."

At the mention of Erasmus Venn, she tilts her head slightly. "I don't know that name. I can check with the Concordance archives, but it'll take time." A beat. "Is that relevant to Hollowfell, or is that a separate question?"

Then, after a pause: "You asked how the Concordance defines anomalies." She gestures vaguely — a half-circle that takes in the tavern, the window, the darkening sky beyond. "Officially? Veil disturbances, planar bleed, temporal displacement, magical suppression — anything that deviates from baseline reality without a natural explanation. That's the handbook answer." Her jaw tightens. "Unofficially? We don't have a clean definition. That's part of why they're hiring people like me to go look instead of sitting in offices arguing about categories."

## Player

I consider this for a moment, my fingers steepled. The magic-dead zone is theoretically fascinating — a suppression field of that magnitude would suggest either a profound arcane anchor or a significant planar bleed. But the Briarwood is where Venn vanished.

"Hollowfell is... intriguing," I say slowly. "A localized antimagic field warrants investigation. But my primary objective remains finding Erasmus Venn. You said you're assigned to the Briarwood."

I lean forward slightly. "What specifically are you investigating there? Venn was studying anomalous biological phenomena in the region before he disappeared. His research notes referenced the Briarwood." My gaze doesn't waver. "If your assignment overlaps with his last known activities, that information would be... highly relevant."

## DM

Wren's expression tightens — not suspicion, exactly, but the focused stillness of someone who's been given pieces and doesn't yet see the picture.

"The Briarwood assignment is monitoring," she says. "Temporal distortions — time moves differently inside the forest. A research team went in three days ago and hasn't returned. We're tracking the anomalies, trying to map where they're concentrated, whether they're spreading." She pauses. "That's the official scope. Observe, record, report."

At the mention of Erasmus Venn's research, her eyes narrow slightly. "I don't know anything about a researcher named Venn. The Briarwood has been wrong for months — people go in and come out... different. Or don't come out at all. But I'm not aware of any specific individual's work being connected to the disturbances."

She studies you for a moment. "You're asking a lot of questions about the Briarwood for someone who's being offered a job at Hollowfell." A beat. "Is there a connection between the two, or are you just thorough?"

The question hangs between you. She's not hostile — she's genuinely trying to understand whether she's missing something. The fire crackles in the hearth. Maret glances over from the bar, then looks away.

## Player

"The connection is Erasmus Venn," I say plainly, as if stating a self-evident fact. "He was my mentor. He disappeared a year ago. His research focused on anomalous biological systems, and his last known trail leads to the Briarwood. I've come here to find out what happened to him."

I pause, processing her words about temporal distortions. "Time moves differently inside the forest. That's... interesting. Venn was investigating living systems that shouldn't exist — organisms adapted to conditions that violate known biological principles. If the Briarwood has temporal anomalies, that could explain certain impossible physiological adaptations."

My spellbook shifts faintly on the table, its pages rustling as if in a nonexistent breeze.

"So yes, there is a connection. Venn went to the Briarwood investigating anomalies. You're investigating anomalies in the Briarwood. The Concordance erased his records after he vanished, which suggests they know something about his work that they haven't shared." I meet her gaze steadily. "I'll take the Hollowfell job. But I would also appreciate any information you can provide about the Briarwood assignment — specifically, the locations where the anomalies are most concentrated."

## DM

Wren goes still when you mention Erasmus Venn. Not the stillness of recognition — she clearly doesn't know the name — but the stillness of someone recalculating.

"The Concordance erased his records," she repeats, and there's a careful neutrality in her voice that might be professional caution or might be something else. "I don't know anything about that. I don't have that kind of access."

She's quiet for a moment. The fire pops. Your spellbook's pages settle.

"But you're taking the Hollowfell job," she says. It's half a question.

When you confirm, something eases in her posture — not放松, exactly, but the subtle shift of a problem finding its shape. "Good. That's what I needed."

She reaches into her coat and produces a folded piece of paper — a rough map, hand-drawn, with a few locations marked in ink. "The Briarwood anomalies cluster around three areas: the Deep Paths — ancient trails through the bramble, some of which lead places that shouldn't exist — the Echo Glade, a clearing where sounds from the past surface — and the Thornwall, a barrier at the forest's edge that grows denser when approached with hostile intent." She pushes the map across the table. "That's what I've been able to map from the perimeter. I haven't gone deep. The research team that went in three days ago was heading for the Echo Glade."

A beat. "If you find anything at Hollowfell that connects to the Briarwood — anything at all — I want to know." She stands, leaving the map on the table. "Report to Brennan Cole at the mine. Tell him Wren sent you. He's expecting someone."

She pauses, hand on the back of the chair. "Your mentor. You think the anomalies took him?"

The question hangs there. She's not asking to be kind — she's asking because if the anomalies are taking people, that changes the scope of her own assignment.

## Player

"I don't know," I say, because that's the truth. "Venn was meticulous. He wouldn't have entered the Briarwood without preparation, and he wouldn't have gone in casually. If the anomalies took him, they did so despite his considerable expertise in surviving hostile environments."

I glance down at the map, studying the three marked locations. "The Echo Glade — a site where temporal echoes manifest audibly. If Venn was researching living systems adapted to impossible conditions, a place where time itself is unstable would be exactly the kind of place he'd investigate."

I look back at her. "I'll report to Brennan Cole at Hollowfell. And if I find anything connected to the Briarwood, you'll know." My fingers trace the edge of the map. "One more question: the research team that went to the Echo Glade three days ago — did they have a Vedalken among them?"

## DM

Wren shakes her head. "No Vedalken. The team was four — two human researchers, a half-elf cartographer, and a guide from the marsh-hamlets. No one matching your description." She pauses. "You think he might have joined them?"

The question makes sense to her — if Venn was investigating the Briarwood, and a research team went in three days ago, the connection is logical. But she doesn't have the answer.

"If he was in the Briarwood three days ago, he's either still in there or he came out and we haven't found him yet." She says it flatly, the way you state weather. "The forest doesn't let people leave easily. Time moves differently inside — an hour can become three, or three can become one. The deeper you go, the worse it gets."

She shifts her weight, ready to leave. "The map I gave you — use it. The Echo Glade is marked. If your mentor was researching temporal anomalies, that's where he'd have started."

A beat. Then, quieter: "Find what happened to him. And find out what Hollowfell is hiding. The anomalies are connected — I can feel it, even if I can't prove it."

She turns toward the door, then stops. "One thing I didn't say earlier. Seraphine Duskward — she's the one who sent me to Greyhaven. She's the First Warden of the Concordance. If you find something at Hollowfell that matters, she's the one who needs to hear it."

She leaves. The door swings shut behind her. The fire crackles. Maret wipes the bar.

Your spellbook is quiet now. The map sits on the table, three locations marked in ink. The stew has gone cold.

The Hollowfell job is accepted. The Briarwood trail is cold but mapped. The night is dark outside.

What does Dallid do?

## Player

I pick up the map, studying the three marked locations. The Deep Paths, the Echo Glade, the Thornwall. My fingers trace the path from Greyhaven to the Briarwood, then east toward the foothills where Hollowfell must be.

I look up at Maret behind the bar. "How far is Hollowfell Mine from here? And is the road safe to travel at night?"

## DM

Maret looks up from the bar, her expression flat. "Few hours east. Follow the old mining road past the bramble line, up into the foothills. You can't miss it — there's a spur of smoke most days from the ore furnaces."

She pauses, wiping a spot on the bar that doesn't need wiping. "Safe enough at night, if you know the road. The bramble doesn't move after dark — it's worse during the day, when the light shifts. But the foothills are cold this time of year, and the road washes out in places. You'd want a lantern, and you'd want to move steady."

She glances at your spellbook, then back at you. "You're heading out tonight?"

The question is practical, not nosy. She's asking because if you're leaving, she'll want to settle your tab.

## Player

"I'll settle my tab," I say, reaching for my satchel. "And yes, I'm heading out tonight. A few hours' travel with a lantern is preferable to waiting until morning."

I count out the coins for my stay and the cold stew, then fold the map carefully and tuck it into my coat. My spellbook closes with a soft rustle and slides into my satchel, its pages settling against my notes and samples. The two-headed gecko on my shoulder shifts, both heads turning toward the door as if sensing the change in intention.

"The old mining road past the bramble line," I repeat, committing the directions to memory. "Thank you."

I stand, adjust my quarterstaff across my back, and move toward the door.

## DM

Maret nods, takes your coins without counting them, and turns back to the bar. The transaction is complete.

The night air hits you like cold water — autumn in the Marches, damp and sharp. The old mining road winds east out of Greyhaven, a track of packed earth and loose stone that climbs gradually into the foothills. Your lantern casts a circle of amber light that bobs with each step. The bramble line falls away behind you. The marsh gives way to rocky ground, then to low hills crowned with scrub pine.

Your spellbook is quiet in the satchel. The gecko shifts on your shoulder, both heads alert.

Two hours pass. The road steepens. The air thins and sharpens with altitude. And then — ahead, a glow. Not lantern light. Furnace light, orange and unsteady, rising from a hollow in the hillside.

Hollowfell Mine.

The surface works are spartan: timber frames, ore carts, a few bunkhouses with smoke rising from their chimneys. Workers move between the structures with the slow, heavy gait of people who have been doing hard labor for too long. They glance at you as you pass — a Vedalken with a spellbook and a quarterstaff, arriving after dark — but no one speaks.

The entrance to the deep tunnels gapes in the hillside like a wound. Timber supports frame the darkness. A cold draft flows outward, carrying the smell of damp stone and something else — a wrongness, a vacancy, like the air itself has been hollowed out.

Your spellbook stirs.

Not the rustle of pages in a draft. Something deeper — a vibration, a resonance, as if the book is trying to align itself with something it can sense but you cannot. The reaction is involuntary and persistent. The book *sees* something in the dark.

The dead zone begins at the tunnel mouth. You can feel it — a threshold where the ambient magic of the world simply stops. Your cantrips would fail here. Your wards would unravel. The air is cold, but the cold is not natural. It is the cold of absence.

Something is wrong here. Something is not natural.

The night is dark. The mine is quiet. Your spellbook is stirring.

## Player

**Player Feedback:**

This session worked really well. A few things that landed:

1. **Wren's introduction** — the way she approached the table, the professional directness, and the slight crack when she mentioned the Concordance's resource limitations. That felt grounded and gave immediate texture to the world.

2. **The layering of mysteries** — Hollowfell as a separate job, the Briarwood as my own trail, and the suggestion they might be connected. It gave me meaningful choices about where to focus, and the information felt earned rather than dumped.

3. **The spellbook's reaction at the tunnel mouth** — ending on that note was perfect. It gave me a clear hook and something Dallid would *have* to investigate. The dead zone described through sensory details (the cold of absence, the hollowed-out air) rather than mechanics made it feel real.

**One thing that felt slightly off:**

- The map Wren provided felt a bit convenient — she handed over detailed information about the Briarwood's anomalies to a stranger she'd just met. Maybe she could have been more guarded, or offered the information as a trade (my findings at Hollowfell for her Briarwood data). That would have reinforced her professionalism and made the relationship feel more transactional, which fits the Concordance's tone.

Overall, though, this was a strong session. Dallid has clear direction, multiple threads to pull, and the Hollowfell entrance is a compelling cliffhanger. Looking forward to the next one.
