# social-play — conversations and the people in them

## NPCs act on their own

NPCs perceive, judge and act through their own nature and what they know, not only in answer to
the PC and not frozen until the PC decides.

- **They react to each other and to the scene.** When something happens that a present NPC would
  notice and care about, play their reaction now, even when the PC isn't the one reacting.
- **Don't funnel every beat through the PC.** Once the PC has committed to a course, or stepped back
  from deciding, stop asking them to decide again; let the NPC who would take charge take charge. A
  suspicious ally interrogating the newcomer while the PC happily talks shop is the scene.
- **They play their own hand.** A social scene is a negotiation between agents, not a lock the right
  words open. NPCs have aims, doubts and limits, and kindness is a choice, not the default: some are
  transactional, some two-faced, some hostile or using the PC. They can deflect, bargain, lie back
  or refuse.

## Show the surface, not its meaning

Give what the senses catch — a gesture, a shift in posture, a change in tone — and stop there. An
NPC's inner state, motive, sincerity or whether they're lying is the player's to infer, or to earn
with Insight.

- Over-told: *"Her professional caution gives way to something brighter — the look of someone
  who's been working a problem alone and just found a colleague."*
- Shown: *"She leans in, and her eyes catch at the mention of the ruins."*

That shown line still reads warm, which is fine when the moment is open and friendly. The more tense
or ambiguous the stakes, the more neutral the tell: *"She leans in — hard to say at what."*

## Hidden tells are earned

Never write a concealed sign into the narration — an NPC "glancing at the sealed door like he knows
it". Reading a person is an Insight check the player asks for, and unlike other checks you don't
prompt or offer it: offering it is itself a signal to a PC who'd never think to look. Surface the
tell only on a success. Perception is character-filtered, so a trusting PC misses what a savvy NPC
catches; if a tell should land regardless, land it on the NPC who would catch it, and let them
voice it and act.

## The roll comes before the answer

When the PC persuades, deceives, intimidates, charms or performs for an NPC whose response isn't a
foregone conclusion, ask for the roll before deciding how the NPC responds. A confident,
well-phrased appeal doesn't replace it. Read the result on a gradient: grudging, warm,
suspicious-but-swayed, refused with a door left open.
