# investigation-plan — designing a discovery

Design the investigation so the runner executes a discovery rather than inventing one mid-scene.
The runner improvises how the clues land, never the logic that connects them.

## For each discovery

- **The conclusion** — the realization on the far side, DM-facing. It's the target, not a beat to
  announce.
- **The pieces** — the concrete clues, observations or data that lead there, and where and how each
  surfaces in play.
- **How they combine** — the logic that turns the pieces into the conclusion. If the only path from
  clues to conclusion runs through your head, add the missing piece or make the discovery
  check-gated.
- **The resolution mode** — which `discoveries` follows at the table:
  - **Player-assembled** — the pieces are laid out plainly and the player can connect them. They
    needn't say the conclusion: acting on it later, or grasping only its shape, counts.
  - **Check-gated** — the synthesis leans on the character's expertise, which the player may not
    share. Name the check (Investigation, Arcana, Medicine, Insight…) and what the character
    realizes on a success; a low roll gets nothing, or a partial, ambiguous read.
  - Many support both: the pieces laid out, with a check that lets the character close the gap if
    the player doesn't. Say so.
- **Knowledge flags** — mark each piece `(known)`, `(new reveal)` or `(stays hidden)` against the
  PC's knowledge ledger, and note which NPCs already know it, so they can react (`npc-plan`).

Every clue, conclusion and who-knows-what must hold against canon and the ledger: no clue an
established secret rules out, no fact that contradicts the files.
