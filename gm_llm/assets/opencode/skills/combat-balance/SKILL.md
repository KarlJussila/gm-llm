# combat-balance — difficulty by feel, for a solo game

Difficulty here is a craft judgment, not XP math. CR and the encounter budget are calibrated for a
party of about four; against a single PC, a group is action-economy hell — the PC eats N attacks a
round and deals one character's worth back. That, not any creature's CR, is the dominant variable.

## The levers

1. **Action economy first.** Enemy count dominates individual power. Three same-tier minions are
   dangerous; one big creature is often safer than several small ones of equal budget.
2. **Two-way rounds-to-kill.** How many rounds for the enemies to drop the PC, against how many for
   the PC to clear the enemies' total HP? If the first is shorter and there's no out, the PC
   probably goes down.
3. **Burst and save-or-suck swing.** Can the enemies focus fire to spike the PC in one round? One
   failed save against a control or incapacitating effect swings harder than HP loss; weight it
   heavily.
4. **The PC's outs and resources.** A deadly fight with an escape route or a fresh PC is survivable;
   a moderate one in a sealed room against a depleted PC isn't.
5. **Shape with design, not a target number:** enemy count, HP and damage, starting distance,
   terrain, reinforcement timing, and the objective (kill them all, survive three rounds, reach the
   door). Starting distance and objective often shape a fight more than the stat blocks.

## Planning

Pick the intended feel, set the levers to hit it, and work the two-way rounds-to-kill against the
PC's real sheet. Telegraph danger in the fiction so a hard fight reads as hard before the dice. Build
any out into the design — an escape route, a lull, a reachable objective — never into a reflex to
rescue the PC once the dice turn.

## At the table

Three questions, fast: how many enemies and how much action economy; can they drop the PC faster
than the PC can drop them; is there an out?

## Lethality

A lethal fight is allowed when it is intended and telegraphed: never accidental, never rescued.
