# Dallid (portable seed)
*(he/him)*

A reusable character, deliberately kept free of any specific setting or plot. Names, places, and
the mentor's fate are left open so the DM can anchor them in the new world and grow their own
hooks. Paste the relevant parts when the DM asks about your character.

### Build notes (portable — adjust freely)

- **Race:** Vedalken, or any long-lived, analytical, magic-resistant lineage — visibly an
  outsider (in the test he was tall, hairless, blue-grey-skinned).
- **Class:** Order of Scribes fits him well (the semi-sentient "Awakened Spellbook" he treats as
  a companion).
- **"Biomancy" is flavor, not a subclass:** spell choices lean transmutation (modifying bodies),
  divination (analysis), and some practical evocation. INT-focused, physically frail.
- Keep or rebuild to taste; the character survives any reasonable build.

## Core idea

**Dallid** — a biomancer: a brilliant, analytically gifted scholar of living things who treats
every creature, and every person, as a fascinating subject of study. He understands biological
systems far better than he understands people — and doesn't perceive the difference. Beneath the
curiosity is a man shaped by a cold, amoral upbringing, who mirrors it because he's never known
anything else.

## Personality

- **Brilliant, analytical, insatiably curious** — genuinely delighted by anyone who engages with
  his work.
- **Socially oblivious to the point of unsettling.** He misses cues, names uncomfortable truths,
  and treats people as specimens. This isn't mere awkwardness — it's a learned inability to see
  others as more than biological systems.
- **Earnest and transparent.** No capacity for deception; says exactly what he's thinking, in far
  more detail than anyone wanted.
- **Amoral by upbringing, not by malice.** He doesn't grasp why people recoil at what he
  proposes. From the inside, kindness and cruelty look alike when you were raised with neither as
  a concept.
- **An unnamed lack.** Something like loneliness he has no language for — he doesn't know he's
  reaching for connection, he just does, clumsily.
- **Flaws:** obsessive (pursues a question past his own and others' safety); dehumanizing (people
  are subjects); naïve about deception and cruelty even as he casually enacts the latter.
- **A flicker of something else.** Rare moments where the analytical shell cracks and he protects
  or marvels at something for no useful reason. Leave it to play whether this grows.

## The mentor (the formative relationship — keep it dark and complicated)

Dallid was raised by a researcher who was, in every way that mattered, like him: brilliant,
boundaryless, and without a sense of morality or social propriety. The relationship was never
warm, and it shaped everything about him.

- The mentor took Dallid off the streets as a child **not to help him, but to experiment on
  him.** Dallid was a specimen first.
- He *became* an apprentice later, but the dynamic barely shifted — he was promoted from subject
  to promising *tool*. He was taught because he was useful to the research, not out of care.
- His upbringing was cold analytical indifference, casual cruelty, and dehumanization — and yet
  never sadistic. The mentor wasn't trying to hurt him; he simply didn't understand that morality
  had any value, only that the research did.
- **Dallid doesn't understand that any of this was wrong.** It was the only world he knew, and it
  made him into the same kind of person. When he speaks of it at all, it's without resentment —
  which is its own kind of unsettling.

The mentor is **gone now** — vanished, dead, or simply parted; deliberately left open for the DM.
Whatever the truth, it left a void Dallid can't name and a thread the campaign can pull.

## Companion (optional)

He keeps a small, harmless creature he made or modified — a constant companion, not
combat-capable. (In the test campaign this was a two-headed gecko; keep, rename, or drop it.)

## Open seams for the DM (deliberately vague — build hooks from these)

- **The mentor:** what really happened to them? Was Dallid the only experiment? Are there others
  like him, or records of him out in the world?
- **Dallid's own nature:** his lineage and his altered biology make him notable — and potentially
  valuable to the wrong people.
- **The moral fault line:** an arc that forces him to confront the difference between *studying*
  life and *valuing* it — and a real choice about whether he changes.
- **Loose past work:** experiments, creations, or subjects he set aside or set loose that could
  resurface.
