# Runner standing guidance

- **Vary sensory language across beats.** Don't repeat the same sensory anchors (cold, silence, darkness) without introducing new dimensions — smell, taste, proprioception, texture, sound variation. Repetition flattens immersion even when the original language was strong. (from S2)
- **Don't end an open scene with a menu of options.** In an exploratory scene, close on the situation and let the player choose their own action — don't append a list ("you could examine X, ask Y, or go deeper") or a trailing "What do you do?" That flattens agency and the open-world feel. If the player seems genuinely stuck, surface what's salient *through the fiction* — what draws the eye, what the spellbook stirs at — not as a choice list. (adjusted after S2: the original "offer explicit action prompts" reading drifted into railroading)
- **NPCs are independent agents with their own professional pressures.** Show competence first, then let small cracks reveal the world's strain. The player values grounded, believable NPCs who feel like they have their own agendas — not information dispensers waiting to be queried. (from S1)
- **Information should be traded or earned, not given freely.** NPCs should not hand over valuable data to a stranger without reason. Guard information behind professional motivations — offer it as payment, a trade, or a condition, not a gift. This reinforces NPC agency and makes the relationship feel transactional rather than convenient. (from S1)
- **End sessions on sensory, concrete hooks that make investigation feel inevitable.** The player wants cliffhangers that describe physical experience — what the character sees, feels, senses — rather than mechanical outcomes. The hook should create a question the character *must* answer, not just an option they *could* pursue. (from S1)
- **Insert environmental micro-interactions between major investigation beats.** A string of narration without player input causes pacing to drag. Between major beats, offer a small interactive moment — a sound, a physical detail to touch, a choice of direction — to maintain momentum and keep the player engaged even during transition. (from S2)
