# The Thornmere Marches

## Vitals
- **Kind / scale:** Frontier province — a stretch of marshy lowlands, scattered settlements,
  and contested wilds. Roughly the size of a small duchy.
- **Terrain:** Brackish marshes, peat bogs, dense bramble forests, and a network of slow
  rivers. Mild but wet climate; long autumns, short summers.
- **Seat of power:** Contested — the Marches were claimed by the Crown of Thorns a generation
  ago, but local lords and freeholds operate with significant autonomy. No single faction holds
  decisive control.
- **Population:** Sparse — homesteads, fishing villages, and a few market towns. The largest
  settlement is Greyhaven, with perhaps three thousand residents.

## Geography

The Thornmere Marches are low-lying and waterlogged, a landscape of dark peat, black-water
rivers, and ancient bramble forests. The terrain makes travel slow and trade difficult — most
goods move by barge along the river network. The eastern edge rises into rocky foothills where
the Marches meet the Ashenmere Wastes; the western edge is coastline, with fishing villages
dotting the shore. The region is beautiful in a bleak, persistent way — everything grows
 stubbornly here.

## Holdings / control

The Crown of Thorns claims sovereignty, but enforcement is thin. Local lords — the Marcher
Lords — govern their own lands with minimal oversight. The [[aetherial-concordance]] maintains
a small outpost in Greyhaven to monitor Veil-activity in the region. The
[[covenant-of-the-sealed-veil]] has no formal presence but is known to operate in the area.

## Notable places

- [[greyhaven]] — the largest town; hub of river trade and the nearest thing to a capital.
- [[the-briarwood]] — an ancient bramble forest where travelers report temporal distortions.
- [[hollowfell-mine]] — a recently reopened mine where magic simply stops working.

## Culture & people

The Marcher folk are practical, suspicious of outsiders, and deeply self-reliant. They
venerate local spirits and ancestors rather than formal gods. The economy runs on peat
harvesting, fishing, and river trade. There's a cultural tension between the old ways —
the freeholds, the spirit-veneration — and the Crown's attempts to impose order. Magic is
feared more than practiced; hedge-witches are tolerated, formal mages are eyed with suspicion.

## Regional history

The Marches were independent for centuries before the Crown's claim. The original Marcher
Lords were war-leaders who consolidated the freeholds against raiders from the Ashenmere
Wastes. When the Crown expanded eastward, the Marcher Lords negotiated autonomy rather than
fight — a pragmatic choice that left them technically subjects but functionally independent.
The Concordance established its outpost after anomalous readings appeared in the Briarwood
three years ago. The readings have been getting worse.

## Connections

Adjacent to the [[ashenmere-wastes]] (east), the Crown of Thorns territory (west), and the
coastal regions (south). Travel to the capital takes two weeks by road, one by river barge.
