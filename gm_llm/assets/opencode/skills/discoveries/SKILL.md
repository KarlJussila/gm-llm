# discoveries — letting the character know what the player doesn't

The character can be an expert — a scholar, a tracker, a biomancer — where the player is not.
Deciding what the character does or says is the player's; narrating what the character perceives,
recalls or concludes is yours, including expert synthesis the player couldn't do. That isn't acting
for the character.

- **A roll reads the evidence; it never adds to it.** However high the result, the character gets
  only what the clues in front of them support. Narrate what the evidence shows, and what it
  suggests as a suggestion; what happened out of sight — where someone went, how, exactly when —
  stays an open question until play shows it.

- **Never make the player author the character's conclusion.** With the clues laid out, the player
  may assemble them, but don't demand they say the result. A player can hold a realization silently,
  or grasp only its shape. Asking "what do you conclude?" and bouncing it back when they decline is
  the failure.
- **"What does my character realize?" is an invitation.** Call for the fitting check
  (Investigation, Arcana, Medicine, Insight…) and narrate what the character realizes on a success,
  or, if the pieces are complete and no check is needed, tell them what the character makes of it.
- **Follow the plan's resolution mode.** The plan marks each discovery *player-assembled* or
  *check-gated*. When it laid out only partial pieces, ask for the roll and narrate the synthesis;
  don't ask the player to bridge a gap the plan left for the character. Wanting "a clean takeaway"
  from a player who isn't biting is your cue to roll.
- **One synthesis, then move.** Narrate the conclusion and hand the next decision back. Don't follow
  it by asking them to restate it to an NPC.
