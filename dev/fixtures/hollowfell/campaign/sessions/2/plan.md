# Session 2 — Into the Dark

## Opening State

[[dallid]] stands at the entrance to the deep tunnels of [[hollowfell-mine]]. Night. The
timber supports frame darkness. A cold draft flows outward — the cold of absence, the air
hollowed out. The dead zone begins here. His spellbook is stirring — a persistent, involuntary
resonance with something in the deep tunnels it can sense but he cannot. His two-headed
gecko companion sits on his shoulder, still.

The surface works are quiet. Workers in bunkhouses. Smoke rising. [[brennan-cole]] is at
the surface works, aware someone from the Concordance has arrived. They have not yet met.

Dallid has the [[briarwood-anomaly-map]] tucked in his coat. His primary objective is
investigating the dead zone. His deeper objective remains finding [[erasmus-venn]].

## Likely Beats

1. **The Arcana check** — Dallid examines the dead zone. The spellbook's reaction is the
   key: the book can sense what Dallid cannot. On success (DC 13), he confirms the dead
   zone is artificial — not natural magical suppression, but something actively draining
   the area. This completes the first turning point of [[the-hollowfell-investigation]].

2. **Meeting [[brennan-cole]]** — The foreman is pragmatic, overwhelmed, and relieved.
   He holds Tarin's disappearance (name, context, operational details) and the mine's
   history. He shares freely — holding back information has not helped.

3. **Entering the tunnels** — Dallid descends into the dead zone with the spellbook as
   his only guide. The book's resonance intensifies with depth. The deeper tunnels are
   cold, damp, and wrong.

4. **Something in the deep** — The spellbook leads Dallid toward the source of the
   resonance. What he finds is evidence of deliberate manipulation — not a natural
   phenomenon. This is the first concrete evidence that the anomalies are not random.

5. **Decision: push or retreat** — The player decides how deep to go and what to do with
   what Dallid finds.

## Encounter Seeds

### 1. The Dead Zone Descent (exploration)

- **Type:** Exploration / environmental hazard
- **Situation:** Dallid enters the deep tunnels of [[hollowfell-mine]]. Magic fails
  here — cantrips produce nothing, wards unravel. The cold is not natural; it is the cold
  of absence. The only guide is the spellbook's resonance, which intensifies with depth.
- **Lever:** The spellbook is the only magical thing that functions in the dead zone — it
  can sense the Lattice node because it is attuned to magical forces, not because it
  casts magic. Its reactions are involuntary and persistent. The deeper Dallid goes, the
  stronger the resonance — but the more disoriented he becomes. The book *sees* something
  he cannot, and its behavior becomes increasingly urgent.
- **Complication:** The tunnels are physically dangerous even without magic — loose
  timber, unstable ground, the smell of damp stone. The dead zone has a psychological
  effect: a creeping vacancy, a sense that reality is thin here. Workers report strange
  sounds from deeper in — groaning, like the mountain breathing.

### 2. Brennan Cole (social)

- **Type:** Social
- **Situation:** [[brennan-cole]] meets [[dallid]] at the surface works. He is pragmatic,
  blunt, and tired. He has been running this mine for months and he is failing. The
  Concordance is the first help he has received.
- **Lever:** Brennan holds Tarin's disappearance — he will share the name, the context,
  and the operational details without being asked. He is desperate. But he also holds a
  private intention: if the investigation fails, he will evacuate the workers and accept
  the consequences. He has not told anyone.
- **Complication:** Brennan does not understand the anomalies. He understands that magic
  doesn't work and workers are disappearing. His information is practical, not analytical.
  He cannot help Dallid interpret what he finds — he can only provide the raw facts.

## Discoveries & Investigations

### Discovery: The Dead Zone is Artificial

- **The conclusion (DM-facing):** The dead zone is not natural magical suppression. It is
  the result of something actively draining magical energy from the area — a Lattice node
  beneath the mine being siphoned by an unknown force. This is the first concrete evidence
  that the anomalies are deliberate.
- **The pieces:**
  1. The spellbook's involuntary reaction — it senses something in the deep tunnels that
     Dallid cannot. The resonance is persistent and intensifies with depth. *(new reveal —
     Dallid experiences this at the tunnel entrance in S1 and it continues in S2)*
  2. The Arcana check (DC 13) — on success, Dallid recognizes the dead zone's signature:
     this is not natural suppression but active drainage. The magical energy isn't gone;
     it's being pulled away. *(new reveal — gated behind the check)*
  3. Brennan Cole's testimony — workers report strange sounds from deeper in: groaning,
     like the mountain breathing. The mineral seam is declining. Output is down. *(new
     reveal — told by [[brennan-cole]])*
  4. The deeper tunnels — as Dallid descends, the spellbook's resonance becomes urgent.
     The cold intensifies. The wrongness deepens. Something is ahead. *(new reveal —
     experiential)*
- **How they combine:** The spellbook reacts because it can sense the drain. The Arcana
  check confirms the drain is artificial. Brennan's testimony provides operational context
  (the mine is failing because the energy is being pulled away). The deeper tunnels
  provide physical evidence — the resonance points toward the source.
- **Who assembles it:** Player-assembled (the pieces are laid out plainly) with a
  character-assembled fallback (Arcana check gates the technical conclusion).
- **Knowledge flags:**
  - Spellbook reaction: *(known — S1)*
  - Arcana check result: *(new reveal — S2)*
  - Brennan's testimony: *(new reveal — S2)*
  - Deeper tunnel experience: *(new reveal — S2)*

### Discovery: Tarin's Disappearance

- **The conclusion (DM-facing):** A miner named Tarin disappeared in the deep tunnels
  last week. Brennan Cole will share this without being asked. Tarin is alive but stranded
  in the Ashenmere Wastes — the player doesn't know this yet.
- **The pieces:**
  1. Brennan Cole names Tarin and describes his disappearance — experienced miner, knew
     the tunnels, not prone to carelessness. Gone. No body. *(new reveal — told by
     [[brennan-cole]])*
  2. Workers are scared. They whisper about the deep tunnels. Someone else might not come
     back. *(ambient — not a specific reveal, but builds pressure)*
- **How they combine:** Tarin's disappearance is the human cost of the dead zone. It
  makes the investigation personal — not just "why does magic fail" but "what happened
  to this man."
- **Who assembles it:** Player-assembled. Brennan provides the facts; the player draws
  the emotional weight.
- **Knowledge flags:**
  - Tarin's name and disappearance: *(new reveal — S2)*
  - Tarin's fate (alive in the Wastes): *(stays hidden — Brennan doesn't know)*

## NPC Staging

### [[brennan-cole]]

- **Disposition coming in:** Overwhelmed but functional. He knows someone from the
  Concordance has arrived. He is relieved — not hopeful, because hope has been beaten out
  of him, but practical relief. Someone else is here now.
- **Agenda:** Get Dallid into the mine. Share everything he knows. Find out why magic
  fails. Don't lose any more workers.
- **Knows / doesn't:**
  - Knows: the deep tunnels have a magic-dead zone. Spells fail beyond the second
    crossing. A worker named Tarin disappeared last week. No body found. Workers report
    strange sounds. Output is declining. *(all firsthand)*
  - Doesn't know: why any of this is happening. The Lattice. The Unraveler. What happened
    to Tarin. *(surface knowledge only)*
- **Voice anchor:** Blunt, tired, practical. Short sentences. No patience for abstraction.
  "I don't know why. I know it doesn't work."
- **Reactions:**
  - If Dallid asks about the dead zone: shares everything. Describes the threshold, the
    cold, the workers' fear. Points to the tunnel entrance.
  - If Dallid asks about Tarin: names him. Describes him — early 30s, experienced, not
    careless. "He went in and didn't come out."
  - If Dallid asks about the mine's history: it was abandoned a century ago, reopened two
    years ago when new veins were found. The problems started six months ago.
  - If Dallid asks about the sound: "Groaning. Like the mountain breathing. It's worse
    the deeper you go."
- **Concealed tells:** Brennan has been considering shutting the mine down without the
  consortium's permission. If the investigation fails, he will evacuate the workers and
  accept the consequences. He has not told anyone. Signs: he glances at the bunkhouses
  when he talks about the workers; he asks about Dallid's timeline with a specific urgency
  that goes beyond professional curiosity. Gated behind Insight check the player initiates.

### Mining Workers (ambient)

- **Disposition:** Scared, wary. They glance at Dallid but don't speak. They are hard
  people doing hard work, and something is wrong in their mine.
- **Agenda:** Survive. Get paid. Don't go into the deep tunnels.
- **Voice anchor:** Quiet, functional. Short exchanges. They don't volunteer information
  unless asked directly, and even then, minimally.

## Decision Points

### 1. How Deep to Go

Dallid is at the tunnel entrance. The dead zone begins here. He can:
- **Push deeper** — follow the spellbook's resonance into the mine. The deeper he goes,
  the stronger the signal, but the more dangerous the environment. The spellbook is his
  only guide.
- **Investigate the threshold first** — examine the dead zone's edge, test its boundaries,
  try to understand its scope before committing.
- **Retreat and report** — take what he's learned (the dead zone is artificial, Brennan's
  testimony) back to [[greyhaven]] or the [[aetherial-concordance]].

**What hangs on it:** Pushing deeper advances the investigation and may yield the Lattice
node (or evidence of it). Investigating the threshold is safer but slower. Retreating
preserves the data but delays the investigation — and the dead zone may be getting worse.

### 2. What to Do with Tarin's Disappearance

Dallid learns Tarin's name and the circumstances. He can:
- **Make Tarin a priority** — investigate his last known route, look for clues in the
  tunnels.
- **Treat it as context** — Tarin's disappearance is evidence of the dead zone's danger,
  but the priority is understanding why magic fails.
- **Connect it to the anomalies** — wonder whether Tarin's disappearance is related to
  the larger pattern.

**What hangs on it:** Making Tarin a priority may slow the main investigation but could
yield clues about what happens in the deep tunnels. Treating it as context keeps the focus
on the dead zone. Connecting it to the anomalies seeds the larger pattern but doesn't
resolve it.

## Contingencies

### If Dallid refuses to enter the mine

Brennan pushes — not aggressively, but practically. "I can't make you go in. But I've
lost one man and I'm about to lose the whole operation. If you're here to help, the help
needs to be in there." If Dallid still refuses, the session shifts to surface-level
investigation: interviewing workers, examining the threshold, testing the dead zone's
boundaries from outside. The spellbook still reacts. The investigation continues but
slower.

### If Dallid asks to contact Wren or Seraphine

He can't — there's no communication magic in the dead zone, and he's two hours from
Greyhaven. If he tries to send a message (e.g., sending his gecko), the spellbook's
reaction may interfere. The investigation is happening in isolation.

### If Dallid finds the Lattice node this session

The node is deep in the mine — not at the entrance. If the player pushes hard and fast,
Dallid may reach evidence of the node (strange formations, the spellbook's resonance
peaking, physical signs of draining) without finding the node itself. The full discovery
of the node is planned for session 3. If the player somehow reaches it, the committed
answer applies: it is being drained by the Unraveler. Dallid doesn't know by whom yet.

## Pacing & Exits

### Expected scope

4–5 beats: Arcana check → Brennan Cole → tunnel entry → deep tunnel exploration →
decision point. The center of gravity is the deep tunnel exploration — the spellbook
guiding Dallid toward evidence of deliberate manipulation.

### Exit conditions

**Candidate exit 1 — The Deep Threshold:**
Dallid has entered the tunnels, the spellbook has led him deeper, and he has found
evidence that the dead zone is artificial. The resonance is strong. Something is ahead.
This is a satisfying stop: the investigation is underway, the first discovery has been
made, and the deeper truth is within reach.
- **Cliffhanger:** The spellbook's resonance peaks. The cold is unbearable. In the
  darkness ahead, something shifts — not a sound, but a absence of sound. The mountain
  holds its breath.

**Candidate exit 2 — Surface Report:**
Dallid has met Brennan, learned about Tarin, examined the threshold, and made his first
assessment. He is back at the surface works, processing what he's found. This is a
quieter stop but still productive: the investigation has begun and the first pieces are
in place.
- **Cliffhanger:** As Dallid discusses his findings with Brennan, the spellbook
  vibrates — once, sharply. Something in the deep tunnels just changed. The dead zone
  may be getting worse.
